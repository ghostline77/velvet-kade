# VK006 — Ghosts in the Heartware

Braindance / psychological mystery. Target: 35–45 minutes. First draft; not implemented. [Dialogue](script/dialogue.md) · [Implementation](plan.md).

Cress, the reseller named in the race ledger, offers bespoke romantic braindances. One preview shows Velvet greeting V on a rainy walkway weeks before their actual meeting. Velvet has no memory of it. V's face could come from a public recording; the intimate pause before Velvet says V's name is harder to dismiss. The first scene lets both characters say what they know before either accuses the other of lying.

They use the isolated reader acquired after VK005. This is recorded material with inspectable staging, not time travel and not evidence that the published first meeting was secretly fake. V enters a compact performance scene while present-day Velvet remains an external guide. A second on-screen Velvet is explicitly the recorded performer, distinguished by context and subtitle speaker labeling. Present-day Velvet can interrupt analysis; she cannot alter the recorded events.

The investigation has three mandatory independent clues across visual, audio and thermal layers. Visual: a reflection shows a different original participant wearing the same sleeve motion as the apparent V. Audio: room tone changes under the spoken name; the city announcement dates the source later than the advertised timestamp. Thermal: the target's face lacks the sensor continuity shown on the body. These establish an edited composite without needing a forensic lecture. Optional clue: a bracelet belongs to a source actor who authorized a commercial shoot but never authorized importing other people's private recordings.

The clip contains a genuine private fragment of Velvet recording a message she never sent, spliced into a commercial base scene. The remembered vulnerability is real; the encounter with V is fabricated. Cress obtained the fragment through the same unauthorized archive market as June's sources, not through secret access to every scene V and Velvet have shared. Velvet chooses whether to explain whom the unsent message was for. The story does not force her to disclose a previous partner as payment for V's trust.

After exiting the braindance, V and Velvet confront Cress at a transfer room. He offers to sell them an “uncontaminated” copy and insists that his customers know the relationships are simulated. The evidence shows the real issue: stolen performances and private memories are sold without the subjects' permission, and the origin is hidden. V can force surrender with the purchase/signature trail, or fight a local guard; the archive controls remain available either way.

Final choice: purge the local intimate recordings after extracting a non-intimate audit trail, or escrow encrypted source recordings to a survivor-controlled recovery process with no public access. Purge loses possible recoveries for other subjects; escrow preserves that chance and an ongoing custody burden. There is no unrestricted leak ending disguised as justice. Both revoke Cress's local sale credentials and document that other copies may exist.

Logs reveal the reseller sold Velvet's old safehouse address with her recordings. Velvet decides it is time to move, rather than waiting for another attacker. The last beat returns to the actual shared past: V and Velvet name one small true thing from dinner. Friendship receives the same reassurance as romance.

Reward: proposed 7,000 eddies recovered from Cress's settlement fund. No fake memory is added as a romantic keepsake. A redacted audit reference becomes a document at the hub; ownership of a stolen clip never becomes a reward.
