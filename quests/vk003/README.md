# VK003 — Love Is a Glitch

Stealth / social infiltration. Target: 30–40 minutes. First draft; not implemented. [Dialogue](script/dialogue.md) · [Implementation](plan.md).

Aster's couples night sells reassurance by the hour. Its behavioral model identifies patrons likely to stay for one more drink, then supplies paid hosts with prompts assembled from private messages. Lonely people are charged for interactions presented as spontaneous. The problem is concealed manipulation and stolen information, not paid company itself.

Velvet has a receipt from VK002 and wants evidence of who is being targeted. V and Velvet book a couples audit package: Aster deliberately excludes solo guests from the demonstration floor where its sales console operates. They agree on cover boundaries before entering. Two friends pretending to date get the same route as interested partners; touching, dancing and kissing are never admission requirements.

The host asks why they are together. Their answer becomes a later joke, not a hidden romance flag. At the dance floor, V can join Velvet at a reviewed workspot, talk at the edge, or use the noise to reach the maintenance corridor. A patron, Edda, is being pressured to authorize another credit extension. Interrupting that upsell produces an optional humane beat and a distraction, but does not force her to publicly recount her private life.

Floor technician Io can be persuaded to issue a maintenance pass after V shows a redacted proof that Aster is buying spoofed identities. Otherwise V uses the service route and access console. Detection changes the route into a conversation with the manager or a contained escape; it never silently fails the whole quest. The mandatory evidence is the billing rule, the source-data policy and the retention schedule, rather than a dump of intimate patron profiles.

Inside the server room, Velvet insists on shutting down live targeting before resolving the archive. Two supported endings: physically destroy the local model and its profiles, or extract redacted audit evidence, publish it and revoke the stolen data credentials. Publishing leaves a documented public trail but may invite attempts to rebuild; destruction stops this installation immediately but leaves less public proof. Both disable the active system. Io's safety and patron redaction are part of either plan, not a third “perfect ending” unlocked by a skill check.

The repair-vendor appendix names a clinic bundle Velvet recognizes. She has experienced a brief unexplained sadness earlier in the night, but does not diagnose herself on the spot. The sequel lead appears in a calm debrief. Payment and completion are independent of whether V agrees with her archive decision.

Guaranteed proposed reward: 6,000 eddies. Callbacks: Io's thank-you note after publication, or an inert circuit board after destruction. If V declined the dinner relationship, Velvet offers a professional cover briefing rather than assuming they are romantically close.
