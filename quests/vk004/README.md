# VK004 — Heartbeat Protocol

Character mystery. Target: 30–40 minutes. First draft; not implemented. [Dialogue](script/dialogue.md) · [Implementation](plan.md).

Velvet asks V to sit with her during an offline implant diagnostic. The sadness at Aster has recurred around sensory triggers she cannot place: a cheap soap, a railway announcement, a song she does not like. She is frightened less by sadness than by being unable to tell which feelings she chose to keep.

A physical maintenance disconnect contains the symptoms. V reads three diagnostic fragments and visits the vendor listed in Aster's documents. Sable Rhee admits that a repair bundle carried an archived affective model of their deceased partner June. They used customer implants as unauthorized test hosts because a private model would not stay active without live input. The story does not require pretending such a model is an actual complete human consciousness; it is a fictional process with uncertain continuity and an observable ability to ask for things.

Sable offers removal, but first wants to preserve the latest process state. Velvet refuses any continued connection while they discuss it. V helps move the conversation to a physically isolated diagnostic speaker, with no network or neural interface. June can speak there briefly; the power supply is stable, so the decision has no artificial countdown.

The emotional center is a three-way conversation. Velvet can recognize June's fear without accepting Sable's claim to use her body. June resents being called a backup and cannot reliably remember the death Sable keeps asking her to explain. Sable must acknowledge that grief gave them no permission to implant strangers. V can ask skeptical questions about personhood without becoming the story's villain.

All endings remove the process and unauthorized permissions from Velvet. She supports keeping an isolated June if June agrees; V may argue for an inert sealed archive or deletion. June can prefer survival while acknowledging that no one owes her neural access. Velvet makes the final hardware decision after hearing V; the player chooses which plan V offers to help carry out. A deletion branch includes June's objection and explicit acknowledgment, never a triumphant “correct” sting. An archive is inert and cannot converse at the later hub. Isolation is a maintained local process with no body or network access, not a restored girlfriend.

After removal, Velvet sits with V outside the clinic. Her first feeling is not magically joy; she is tired and hungry. Sable provides written evidence of the unauthorized bundle and a recall list stripped of patient names. An export-queue entry indicates the archived inputs were sold to a memory reseller; that lead waits for VK006. Niko, a courier who carried clinic gear, offers a race purse that can fund a private archive reader for the investigation in VK005.

Reward: proposed 5,000 eddies drawn from the clinic refund settlement, paid in every ending. Velvet's continued trust is based on whether V respects her chosen boundary, not on agreeing with her interpretation of June. Friendship and romance both get the quiet closing scene.
